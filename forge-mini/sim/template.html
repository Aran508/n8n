Can it do tiny work?

Small assembly, soldering and the underside

Each level is computed by forge precision from the arm geometry and each servo's angle error. Typical is the usual case; worst is when every joint errs the same way. Lead-in chamfers, funnels and spring-loaded tools let a part find its hole even when the arm is a little off.

Working underneath a product

The arms only work from above, so the centre fixture is a flip fixture: a trunnion that turns the part 180° in 1.6 s. The planner releases clamps, keeps every arm out while it turns, and knows the part is upside down, so it refuses to unload it until it is turned back. That covers soldering through-hole legs, screws from below and marking the bottom. Watch it in the LED bar project.

A complete product

Yes, if every step can be done from above or after a flip, and the parts come in a kit: printed parts, modules, screws, through-hole parts, wires to pads. Ready-made boards (an Arduino Nano, an OLED module) go in as whole parts. Fine-pitch chips and cables that must be routed through tight channels are still jobs for a person or a bigger machine.

Tool set

Ten tools, two racks, one bit block

Tools hang through holes in a raised shelf, like a reamer stand. Neighbouring arms share a rack: arm 1 and arm 3 use the left one, arm 2 and arm 4 the right one. The spindle picks its cutters from the bit block, so the same tool reams, drills, chamfers or drives screws. The soldering iron is a USB-C mini iron on its own thin cable; the tool signal only drives its solder feeder and switches the heater.

The tool changer

Every wrist ends in the same coupler, and every tool carries the matching plate. The arm lowers onto a tool in the rack, an SG90 turns a cam to lock it, and the ESP32 reads the tool's ID resistor before the arm leaves the rack. A wrong or missing tool stops the run.

±0.05 mmrepeatability of 3 balls in 3 V-grooves
22 mmwrist axis to coupler face
10 gcoupler on the arm, with magnets
  1. Arm lowers onto the tool with the latch open. The balls drop into the grooves and the magnet pulls the plates flat.
  2. The latch servo turns 85°, and its hook locks under the tool plate.
  3. The ESP32 reads the ID pin. A gripper reads 1 kΩ, the spindle 10 kΩ, nothing at all reads open.
  4. The SIG pin now drives that tool: gripper jaw, pump, spindle speed or glue plunger.
  5. To return a tool, the arm lowers it into its slot, opens the latch and lifts away.

Arm design

All four arms are identical: base yaw, shoulder, elbow and wrist, with the tool always pointing straight down. The tool's length becomes the last link, so the kinematics change with every tool change and the planner keeps track.
